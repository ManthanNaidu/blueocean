Extinction Crisis

Over 1 million marine species are at risk of extinction due to human activities. Coral reefs, often called the "rainforests of the sea," are dying at alarming rates.

Main Causes:

  • Pollution (plastic, chemicals, oil spills)
  • Climate change (warming, acidification)
  • Overfishing
  • Loss of habitat

What can we do?

  • Support marine protected areas
  • Reduce plastic use
  • Participate in cleanups
  • Advocate for sustainable fishing
Ocean Pollution

Every year, over 8 million tons of plastic end up in our oceans. Microplastics, oil spills, and chemical runoff threaten marine life and human health.

Major Sources:

  • Single-use plastics
  • Industrial and agricultural runoff
  • Untreated sewage
  • Oil spills

How You Can Help:

  • Reduce plastic use and recycle properly
  • Participate in local cleanups
  • Support policies that protect waterways
Overfishing

Overfishing has reduced fish populations to unsustainable levels, threatening food security and ocean health. Many species are now endangered, and entire ecosystems are at risk.

Consequences:

  • Loss of biodiversity
  • Disruption of food chains
  • Economic impact on fishing communities

Solutions:

  • Support sustainable seafood choices
  • Enforce fishing regulations
  • Promote alternative livelihoods for fishermen
Endangered Ocean Species
  • Hawksbill Sea Turtle: Critically endangered due to illegal trade and habitat loss.
  • Vaquita: The world’s rarest marine mammal, with fewer than 10 left in the wild.
  • Blue Whale: The largest animal on Earth, threatened by ship strikes and entanglement.
  • Hammerhead Shark: Overfished for fins, now endangered.

How to Help:

  • Support conservation organizations
  • Reduce plastic and chemical pollution
  • Promote sustainable seafood
  • Participate in awareness campaigns
